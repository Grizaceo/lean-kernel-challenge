INTERPRETATION, stated as what the data support:
  1. The cost is a STEP FUNCTION of bit-width, not linear. Payload size
     buys nothing below 63 bits: the lower plateau spans
     19 widths from 4 to 62 bits and moves 0.54%, i.e. within the 0.43% instrument.
  2. The step sits at 63 bits: 62 bits costs 234,149, 63 costs 241,691 (+3.17% vs the lower plateau), and 64 costs
     248,015. That is exactly where a Nat stops fitting
     the kernel's single machine word and must be carried as a digit array.
     The sweep locates the crossing to within one bit and does NOT by
     itself prove the mechanism.
  3. Above the step the curve is flat to within 1.3x the instrument
     (0.55% over 10 widths, 0.43% instrument), so a
     residual per-bit marginal cost is at most of that order and is NOT
     resolved by this sweep. The four-point table's apparent
     147 instr/bit was the 63-bit step
     smeared across a linear fit, not a rate.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE COMPETITION COST MODEL
  Payload width is a near-irrelevant lever. The only width decision that
  matters is staying under 63 bits, worth 6.0% of a single
  theorem replay. Everything else that costs time is the number of
  theorem declarations replayed, each paying the full plateau cost.
